Chapter 1 · The long-run model

Where does the price sit in its long story?

What bitcoin is.

Bitcoin is money for the internet. No company runs it, and no boss can print more: only 21 million will ever exist. It lives on many computers at once. You can own a whole coin, or a small piece.

Where are we now?

Nine colored bands stack around one line: bitcoin's long-run growth, fit to every daily price on record. Low bands: far under its own past growth. High bands: far over it.

What the colors mean.

Tap a color on the chart, or a band in the list. Each band tells you how far from the line it sits, how many days the price has lived there, and what the next year did.

So, does this mean buy?

No. The chart shows where the price sits inside its own past pattern. It cannot say buy, and it cannot say skip. No chart can.

What it can do: show what happened the other times the price sat where it sits now. See the band list above, or scrub the chart.

Why we think it climbs on. A belief, not a fact.

Pick any start day: four years on, the price stood higher. Every time, so far. Why might it go on? Only 21 million will ever exist, and new coins come slower every four years.

Reading the falls…
How each fall is measured

The top is a daily close. The fall runs to the lowest daily close after it. Days back to the top counts from the top to the first close at or above it again. Daily closes can miss a swing inside one day, so a fall here can read smaller than the worst hour of it.

It swings hard. We could be wrong.

It has fallen by half or more seven times since 2011. It could fall and stay down. The climb is a pattern, not a law.

Chapter 5 · Sources & method

How do we know any of this?

Sources & method.

The model

Rainbow model, our own fit, version 1. One line fit to every daily close on record:

ln(price) = a + b × ln(days since Jan 3, 2009)

Reading the fit…

Nine bands stack on the line. Each is the same step wide: about 1.35 times the price below it. The ladder runs from 0.47 to 7.03 times the line.

First drawn by azop in 2014, on trolololo's growth line. Blockchain Center made it famous. This fit is our own, on the same public prices. A fit describes the past. It is not a forecast.

Reading the sources…

What to watch for

Prices here are daily closes. A swing inside one day can be bigger than the day shows. In June 2011, one exchange flashed to one cent for a few minutes. The daily closes say that fall was 93%. Both are true, of different things.

The fit score (R²) grades how well the line hugs the past. It says nothing about next year.

Bitcoin's share of all crypto depends on who counts the coins. Sites disagree by a point or two. Ours comes from CoinGecko, named on the card.

The Fear & Greed score is one outside index, made by Alternative.me. We show it with its maker's name next to it, and we do not fold it into any other number here.

The model explorer stretches the model's own lines forward. Those lines are labelled as the model's, never as a forecast.
