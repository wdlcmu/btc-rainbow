What is a bear market in bitcoin?

A bear market is a long, deep fall from a price top. Writers often draw the line at 20% down.

Bitcoin's real bear markets went far past that line. They fell 53% to 93%, and they lasted years.

The falls that earn the name

Since 2011, the price has fallen by half or more seven times. These are the falls people call bitcoin bear markets:

WhenThe fall
2011$34 to $2, down 93%
2013$231 to $68, down 71%
2013 to 2015$1,137 to $172, down 85%
2017 to 2018$19,280 to $3,232, down 83%
Spring 2021$63,554 to $29,790, down 53%
2021 to 2022$67,562 to $15,760, down 77%
2025 to now$124,777 to $58,534, down 53%, still open

A crash is not the same thing

A crash is about speed: sharp falls in days or weeks.

A bear market is about length: the long grind of lower prices that can follow. The 2018 crash fell fast at first. The bear market ground on for a year.

How long they last

The big ones took about a year to reach the low. The climb back to the old top took 2 to 3 years in all.

What this page is not saying

Each past bear market ended. That is a fact of the past.

The word names the past. It does not time the next turn, and it gives no buy or sell answer.
