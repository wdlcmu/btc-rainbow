The rainbow chart, in plain words.

Think of marks on a wall, showing a child's growth year by year. This chart does that for price.

One line is fit to all past prices. Nine bands of color wrap the line. Low bands are blue. High bands are red.

How the line is made

The line is fit to all past days at once, by least squares. The form is:

ln(price) = -37.283 + 5.597 x ln(days since Jan 3, 2009)

It uses 5,917 days of prices from blockchain.info, Aug 18, 2010 to Oct 29, 2026.

What the bands mean

Each band is the same width in log space: 0.3. Deep Blue is the base band. Red is the top band.

The nine bands, low to high: Deep Blue at the base, Red at the top.

Blue days are far more common than red days — a fact of the past data, not a tip.

Where the chart comes from

The rainbow is a public chart. A Reddit user, azop, drew the first one in 2014. A forum user, trolololo, drew the growth line. Blockchain Center made the chart famous. The line on this page is our own fit. It uses the same public price history.

Where the price sits now

On Oct 31, 2026, one coin cost $77,471. The line said $141,853 — so the price sat in Deep Blue, 45% below the line.
