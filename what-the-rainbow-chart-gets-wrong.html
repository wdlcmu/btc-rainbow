What the rainbow chart gets wrong.

The hard questions about this chart. Answered with our own numbers.

591 days below the bottom band. 57 days above the top.

Every daily close on record: 5,921 days, Aug 18, 2010 to Nov 2, 2026. And the line was drawn with hindsight.

What is it wrong about?

The line is a curve fit to the past. It is not a law. No force makes the price follow it.

And it was fit knowing the ending. Fit the same line using only prices through 2013, then judge the years after. The price spent 3,249 of the next 4,689 days below the whole ladder. That is 69% of the time. Drawn in 2013, the chart failed. Drawn today, it hugs. The difference is hindsight.

The 0.96 fit score flatters it, too. On log scales, any price that rose a lot scores high. Judge by distance instead: half of all days closed at least 1.65 times away from the line, over or under. Only 530 of 5,921 days closed within 10% of it.

The chart is also silent where people ask most. It says nothing about why the price moved. It says nothing about next month.

Why does it bend?

One rule sits under the chart. The line rises with age to the power 5.6. Each time bitcoin’s age doubles, the line climbs about 48 times.

Early on, age doubled in months, so the line rockets. Now an age doubling takes about 18 years, so the same rule crawls. Same rule, slower clock. That is the bend.

The math way, the curve is not a curve. Plot price in logs against time in logs, and it runs straight. Spread the years out evenly, as charts do, and the straight line bends.

The bands are the line times fixed steps. Each band is 1.35 times the band below it. So the bands bend with the line, and hold the same width in percent.

Has the price ever escaped the bands?

Yes: 648 of 5,921 days, the count in the card above.

Above the top: 57 days, all early. In June and July 2011 the price lived over the top for 27 days. On June 11, 2011 it closed at $33.80, with the top of the ladder at $14: 2.3 times the top. In late 2013 it escaped again, 30 days, peaking at $1,134 against a $729 top on Nov 30, 2013. No day has closed above the top band since Jan 13, 2014.

Below the bottom: 591 days. The price lived under the ladder for 309 days across 2010 to 2013, and 240 more across 2015 and 2016. The longest stretch out ran 89 days, Oct 25, 2012 to Jan 21, 2013. The deepest day: Oct 7, 2010, a 6-cent close at 38% of the bottom edge.

Since then, only dips. One day in the March 2020 crash: $4,830 against a $4,944 bottom edge. Ten days at the November 2022 bottom, and 26 more that December and January, never 5% under the edge. Five days in June and July 2026, a hair under.

Is it a prediction?

No. It is a ruler laid over history. It says where the price sits inside its own past. Nothing more.

Being low in the band ladder has not reliably predicted what came next. A low band is a fact about today, read against the past. It is not a fact about next month.

What would break it?

Our line is refit over every close on record each time the data rebuilds. With 5,921 days behind it, one new day barely moves it. So a break cannot hide: the line will not quietly chase the price.

A break looks like this. The price lives outside the ladder for months, not days. Or years pass with growth nowhere near the fitted slope.

If that comes, the honest move is to say so. The fit score on the method page would fall. The bands would no longer hold the past they were drawn from.
